# Gym Progression Matrices & Intensity Scaling Tree

Progress in an iron-based strength program relies entirely on managing **mechanical tension, volume accumulation, and metabolic fatigue**. Use this matrix to scale intensity variables accurately.

---

## 1. Compound Strength Progression Trees (The Big 3 / 4)
When performance plateau steps occur, manipulate parameters systematically down the tree rather than adding arbitrary weight.

```text
Step 1: RPE-Based Volume Expansion (Add sets/reps inside a stable intensity zone)
   └── Step 2: Eccentric Accentuation (Incorporate 3-4s slow lowering phases)
         └── Step 3: Concentric Tempo Alterations (Incorporate explicit dead-stops or pauses)
               └── Step 4: Absolute Load Advancement (Increase mass on the bar by 2.5% to 5%)
                     └── Step 5: Advanced Intensifiers (Drop-sets, Cluster sets, Rest-Pause)
```

| Progression Stage | Primary Coaching Cues | Criteria for Adding Mass to Bar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1: Technical Mastery**| Control the eccentric, lock out hips, pack lats | Hit all planned sets at target RPE without form breakdown |
| **Stage 2: Tempo Pauses** | Pause 2 seconds at the bottom/sticking point | Complete full volume blocks with zero shifting velocity |
| **Stage 3: Load Progression** | Drive bar with max intent through concentric | Cleanly hit top-end rep range with 1-2 reps left in reserve (RIR) |

## 2. Intensity Management: The RPE / RIR Conversion Table
Use this table to calibrate client self-reporting during working sets.

| RPE Scale | Reps in Reserve (RIR) | Description of Effort |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **10** | 0 | Absolute maximum effort; could not perform any more repetitions or add load. |
| **9.5** | 0-1 | Could not perform more reps, but could possibly handle slightly more weight. |
| **9** | 1 | Could definitely perform exactly 1 more clean repetition. |
| **8** | 2 | Could perform 2 more clean repetitions before failure. |
| **7** | 3 | Weight is moving fast; warm-up or high-power developmental load. |

---

## 3. Deload & Fatigue Management Protocol
Trigger a standard structural deload week if the client exhibits 3 or more of these criteria:
* [ ] Decreasing grip strength or bar velocity over two consecutive weeks.
* [ ] Persistent joint discomfort or morning resting heart rate elevations (>5 bpm over baseline).
* [ ] Persistent subjective RPE increase on standard working weights.
* **Deload Execution:** Reduce total working sets by 50% and reduce working intensity (load) by 10% to 15%. Keep exercise selection identical.
